Accident Weed App – Image Task List (task_image.md)
This document defines the image assets to be generated for the Accident Weed–based safety app.
All images should share a consistent visual style (colors, line weight, typography).

Style: flat vector, clean, mobile‑first, friendly but professional
Color: soft greens for plant, brown for soil/roots, accent colors for card categories
Language: visual labels can be prepared with text placeholders; actual Korean text will be applied in the app UI
1. Main Accident Weed Concept Illustration
ID: img_main_accident_weed

Purpose:
Onboarding and education screens, explaining the full Accident Weed concept (flower–leaf–root metaphor).

Prompt (English):
“Flat vector illustration of a stylized green plant shaped like a tree or weed, growing from brown soil. At the top of the plant, several bright yellow flowers labeled ‘Injury’ and ‘Illness’ represent visible outcomes. On the left side of the stem, multiple green leaves labeled with unsafe conditions such as ‘No guards on machines’, ‘Broken tools’, ‘Chemical leak’, ‘Crowded workspace’, ‘Poor lighting’. On the right side of the stem, multiple green leaves labeled with unsafe behaviors such as ‘Ignoring safety rules’, ‘Rushing work’, ‘Not using PPE’, ‘Horseplay’, ‘Lack of focus’. Below the soil, thick roots spread out horizontally with red labels such as ‘Lack of safety training’, ‘No follow‑up after accidents’, ‘Insufficient budget for safety equipment’, ‘Weak supervision’, ‘Unsafe workspace design’. A clean, simple infographic style, white background, Korean and English text placeholders on the labels, suitable for a safety training mobile app.”

2. Blank Accident Tree Template (No Labels)
ID: img_blank_tree_template

Purpose:
Base image for the drag‑and‑drop interface, without any text or icons. Cards will be dragged onto this tree.

Prompt (English):
“Simple flat vector illustration of an abstract green plant shaped like a tree or weed, growing from brown soil. The plant has a single main stem, several empty flower spots at the top, multiple blank leaves on the left and right of the stem, and branching roots visible below the soil. No text, no labels, and no icons on the plant. Use soft green for the leaves, small yellow flower placeholders at the top, and warm brown for the soil. Clean, minimal design on a white background, optimized as a neutral template for drag‑and‑drop elements in a mobile safety app.”

3. Flower Result Cards (Injury Types) – Drag Items
ID: img_cards_flowers_results

Purpose:
Draggable “flower” cards that represent accident outcomes.

Prompt (English):
“Set of flat vector icon cards representing different injury outcomes as stylized flowers. Each card is a rounded rectangle with a white background, a soft yellow border, and a small yellow flower icon at the top left. Below the icon, leave a clear text label area. Example label placeholders: ‘Burn’, ‘Cut’, ‘Fracture’, ‘Bruise’, ‘Chemical Exposure’, ‘Equipment Damage’, plus one card labeled ‘Other’. Use a friendly sans‑serif font and high contrast. Arrange the cards in a simple grid on a white background, ready to be sliced into individual assets for drag‑and‑drop in a mobile app.”

7. Drag Target Highlights (Tree Zones)
ID: img_tree_dropzones_highlighted

Purpose:
Overlay version of the tree that shows where users can drop cards (flower zone, left leaves zone, right leaves zone, root zone).

Prompt (English):
“Flat vector illustration of the blank Accident Weed tree with its drop zones highlighted. Show subtle colored halo or dashed outlines over the flower area at the top, the left leaves area, the right leaves area, and the root area below the soil. Each zone has a small text label such as ‘Drop flower cards here’, ‘Drop condition cards here’, ‘Drop behavior cards here’, and ‘Drop root cause cards here’. Use semi‑transparent overlays and soft accent colors so the tree remains clearly visible. White background, designed as an instructional overlay for a drag‑and‑drop interface.”

8. Hand + Drag Gesture Icon Set
ID: img_icons_hand_drag_set

Purpose:
Small icons to visually indicate dragging, long‑press, and dropping actions.

Prompt (English):
“Small flat vector icon set showing drag gestures. Include:

1) A hand icon with one finger pressing on a card.
2) A hand dragging a card upward with a curved arrow.
3) A card hovering above a target area with a downward arrow.
Keep the style consistent with the app (rounded shapes, simple strokes, no detailed anatomy). Use soft gray for the hand outline and teal or green arrows. Transparent background for each icon so they can be overlaid on UI elements.”
9. Drag Instruction Overlay Screen
ID: img_overlay_drag_instruction

Purpose:
Full‑screen help overlay that explains how to perform drag & drop on the tree.

Prompt (English):
“Flat vector illustration of a mobile phone screen with the Accident Weed tree in the upper half and one flower card at the bottom being dragged upward by a hand icon. Add a large arrow showing the drag direction from the card toward the top of the tree. Use a semi‑transparent overlay background with minimal instruction text such as ‘Long‑press a card and drag it onto the tree’ as a placeholder. Clean, modern look, white and light gray base colors with green accents, suitable as an in‑app tutorial screen.”

10. Onboarding Illustration – From Blame to System
ID: img_onboarding_blame_to_system

Purpose:
Onboarding visual that contrasts “blaming the worker” vs “finding system roots”.

Prompt (English):
“Flat vector onboarding illustration split into two panels. Left panel: a simple worker character standing next to a very small plant with only one leaf; above the character is a red speech bubble saying ‘It’s the worker’s fault’ with a red cross icon, suggesting a blame culture. Right panel: the same character now looking at a full Accident Weed diagram with flowers, leaves, and visible roots labeled ‘System, Training, Budget, Culture’. Above is a green speech bubble saying ‘Let’s find the root causes’ with a check mark icon. Use bright, friendly colors and simple shapes, suitable for a safety education mobile app.”
